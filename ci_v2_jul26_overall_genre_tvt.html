What this measures: total view-time per genre across everything exposed devices watched post-exposure — not just the 36 held-back titles. The ratio = Hold TVT ÷ Control TVT. At the whole-catalog level this is expected to sit near 100%: holding back 36 titles is a tiny fraction of total viewing, and suppressed demand largely substitutes to other titles in the same genre. A ratio meaningfully below 100% means the holdback caused a net loss of viewing in that genre (true incrementality — demand wasn't fully recovered). Above 100% suggests spillover / substitution into that genre.

Colors: <99% net loss 99–101% flat >101% net gain. Contrast with the within-36-titles segmentation report, where hold was only ~47% of control for the held-back titles themselves.

Viewer counts are distinct devices per arm (each arm ≈ 14.68M exposed).

Significance: the Welch t / p-value columns test whether mean per-exposed-device genre TVT differs between arms (mean taken over the full ~14.68M-device arm, so non-viewers count as zeros — this is the ITT-style test). p (BH-FDR) applies a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across all 36 genres. "Significant? = yes (FDR)" survives correction; "borderline" is raw p<0.05 only (likely false-positive after correcting for 36 tests).

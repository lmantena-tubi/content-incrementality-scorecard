Content Incrementality v6b — Rate vs Pre-Period Gap (with 95% CI)

50 titles × 4 viewer segments · 28-day window · Error bars = 95% confidence intervals · Filled = significant, hollow = not significant

Key takeaway — v6b fixes pre-period TVT calculation: With the corrected matching, the pre-period gap is now ~0 hours (was −2 to −60 hrs in v6). As a result, Simple and DiD now converge (medians 4.58× vs 4.60×), confirming that v6's DiD inflation was entirely caused by the negative pre-gap. Neither method now correlates with pre-gap (r = −0.11 and −0.13).

Additive Proportional (median 1.15×) and DiD Additive Proportional (median 1.16×) are the most conservative — they attribute only title T's proportional share. These are the most defensible per-title estimates. However, 42% of title×segment combinations show rates below 1 (cannibalization), and many CIs cross 1.

Simple and DiD (medians ~4.6×) assign the full treated-vs-control gap to the title. These capture the total ecosystem lift associated with a title's viewers, but overstate any single title's causal contribution when viewers watch multiple titles.

How the methods compare: All four methods estimate how much additional off-title viewing each hour of on-title viewing generates. They all use the control group as a counterfactual, but differ in scope and whether they use the pre-period.

Simple computes the post-period off-title TVT difference between treated and control viewers. Additive Proportional scales this by weight_T — the title's share of a viewer's total on-title viewing time (tvt_on_T / tvt_on_all_titles), typically 5–20%. This is the most conservative method because it prevents double-counting across titles.

DiD differences out each group's pre-to-post change to isolate the title's causal effect. DiD Additive Proportional applies weight_T to DiD. In v6b, with the pre-gap near zero, DiD and Simple agree — validating both approaches when matching is clean.

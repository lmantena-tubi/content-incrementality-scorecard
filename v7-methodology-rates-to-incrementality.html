Methodology · rate computation

From Pair-Level Deltas to Incremental TVT

How matched-pair off-title viewing differences become a winsorized additively proportional rate and, ultimately, incremental TVT hours per title.

v7 pipeline (April 2026) · 400 MOVIE titles × 4 viewer segments
TL;DR

Input: N matched pairs per (title, segment) cell. Each pair has: treated off-title sec, control off-title sec, treated on-title sec, and engagement weight.

Engagement weight: weight_T = on_title_sec(T) / total_post_tvt — fraction of the viewer's total post-period viewing that went to title T. Scope-independent; bounded [0, 1].

Winsorize: Clip delta and on-title TVT at P1/P99 per cell.

Rate: 1 + Σ(delta_wz × weight) / Σ(on_title_wz)

Incremental TVT: rate × april_segment_tvt

Rate = 1.0 is 100% incremental. Rate < 1 means some displacement. Rate > 1 means the title drove additional off-title engagement beyond its own hours.

§1 · Overview

The chain from pairs to incremental hours

Each matched pair produces a raw delta in off-title viewing. A sequence of transformations converts those raw deltas into the final reported number: incremental TVT per title × segment.

The pipeline runs this chain twice—once over a 28-day post-period and once over a 7-day post-period—producing two independent incrementality estimates per title × segment cell. Both are winsorized. The final combined summary table holds all four rate variants (28d original, 28d winsorized, 7d original, 7d winsorized). The results dashboard reports winsorized rates exclusively.

The core identity: Incremental TVT = Σseg( add_prop_rateseg × segment_tvtseg ) The rate is a multiplier applied to segment-level TVT. When rate = 1.0, the title is 100% incremental (no displacement). When rate = 0.8, 80% of the title's TVT is incremental.
§2 · Pair-level delta

Off-title viewing difference between treated and control

For each matched pair (treated viewer i, control viewer j) observing title T, we decompose post-period TVT into on-title (time watching title T) and off-title (time watching everything else on Tubi).

Simple delta (off-title): simple_delta_off_T = treated_off_title_sec − control_off_title_sec

This measures displacement. A negative delta means the treated viewer watched less off-title content than their control—suggesting the title displaced some off-title viewing. A positive delta means the title's presence was associated with more off-title viewing (the title pulled the viewer to the platform and they watched other content too).

Separately, we record on-title post TVT (tvt_on_T_post): the treated viewer's consumption of title T in the post-period. This becomes the denominator of the rate formula.

Post-period windows

28d: [event_ts, event_ts + 28 days) for treated viewers (anchored on their completion timestamp). [event_date midnight, event_date + 28) for controls (midnight-anchored since controls lack a precise event timestamp).

7d: Same pattern, 7-day window. Captures immediate displacement only; wider CIs but cleaner causal identification.

§3 · Engagement weights

Handling viewers matched to multiple titles

A single treated viewer can complete multiple titles in the same month. Each completion produces a matched pair. Without correction, that viewer's off-title delta would be fully attributed to every title they were matched to—double-counting displacement.

Engagement weight: weight_T = treated_post_on_title_sec(T) / treated_post_tvt_total_sec Fallback when total post-period TVT is zero: weight_T = 1.0.

The denominator is the viewer's total TVT (on-title + off-title) in the post-period window for that specific title's event timestamp. It answers: what fraction of this viewer's total post-period viewing went to title T?

A viewer who spent 5% of their 28d post-window watching title T gets weight_T = 0.05. A viewer who spent 80% of their time on title T gets weight_T = 0.80. The weight is bounded [0, 1] by construction but does not sum to 1 across matched titles—each title's weight is computed independently from that title's own post-period window.

This definition is scope-independent: adding or removing titles from the pipeline does not change any existing title's weight. When the rate formula multiplies delta × weight_T, it allocates displacement proportionally to each title's share of the viewer's total engagement. The 28d rates use 28d-based weights; the 7d rates use independently computed 7d-based weights.

§4 · Winsorization

Clipping outliers at the 1st and 99th percentile

Raw deltas include extreme values—a control viewer who binged 200 hours off-title, or a treated viewer who watched nothing at all in 28 days. These outliers inflate standard errors and can dominate the rate for smaller cells.

Winsorization clips two quantities per (title_id, user_segment) cell:

simple_delta_off_T numerator input

Clipped to [P1, P99] within each title × segment cell. Extreme positive deltas (treated watched far more off-title) and extreme negative deltas (treated watched far less) are bounded. Result: delta_off_T_wz.

tvt_on_T_post denominator input

Clipped to [P1, P99]. Prevents a single heavy rewatcher from dominating the denominator. Result: tvt_on_T_post_wz.

weight_T is not winsorized—it is already bounded [0, 1] by construction.

Why per-cell percentiles

Percentiles are computed within each (title, segment) cell. A Power Viewer cell for a blockbuster has a different TVT scale than a New (M1) cell for a niche title. Per-cell bounds adapt to each cell's distribution.

§5 · Rate formula

The additively proportional (add_prop) rate

The rate answers: what fraction of this title's on-title viewing represents net-new platform engagement?

Winsorized add_prop rate: add_prop_rate_wz = 1 + Σ(delta_off_T_wzi × weight_Ti) / Σ(tvt_on_T_post_wzi) summed over all pairs i in the (title, segment) cell.

Breaking this down:

  • Σ(delta_off_T_wz × weight_T) — total weighted displacement across all pairs. Negative when the title displaced other viewing; positive when it drove additional viewing.
  • Σ(tvt_on_T_post_wz) — total on-title viewing across all pairs. The "investment" the title received.
  • The ratio — displacement per unit of on-title viewing. A ratio of −0.15 means each hour of on-title viewing displaced 0.15 hours of other content.
  • Adding 1 — converts from displacement ratio to an incrementality multiplier. Displacement of −0.15 becomes rate = 0.85.
Rate = 1.0: no displacement. All on-title viewing is net-new to the platform (100% incremental). Rate = 0.80: the title displaced 20% of its on-title hours from other content; 80% is incremental. Rate = 1.15: the title drove 15% more platform viewing than its own on-title hours.
§6 · Incremental TVT

Scaling the rate to actual segment-level viewing

The rate is a unit-free multiplier. To convert it to hours, multiply by the title's actual segment-level TVT in the reporting month (April 2026):

Incremental TVT (title × segment): incr_tvt_hrs = add_prop_rate_wz × april_segment_tvt_hrs

Title-level incremental TVT sums across segments:

Title-level incremental TVT: incr_tvt_title = Σseg( add_prop_rate_wz(seg) × april_segment_tvt_hrs(seg) )

The incrementality ratio is incremental TVT divided by title TVT. On the scatter plot (Tab 2 of the results dashboard), the diagonal line represents ratio = 1.0 (100% incremental). Points below the diagonal have ratio < 1; points above have ratio > 1.

§7 · Confidence intervals

Standard error and CIs at two levels

The pipeline computes CIs in two units: hours per viewer and rate-level.

Per-viewer hours CI: add_prop_per_viewer_hrs_wz = AVG(delta_off_T_wz × weight_T) / 3600 SE_hrs = STDDEV(delta_off_T_wz × weight_T) / √n / 3600 CI = per_viewer_hrs ± 1.96 × SE_hrs
Rate-level CI: SE_rate = STDDEV(delta_off_T_wz × weight_T) × √n / Σ(tvt_on_T_post_wz) CI = rate ± 1.96 × SE_rate

The rate-level SE has √n in the numerator because it represents uncertainty about the sum of deltas (which scales with √n × SD) divided by the fixed total on-title denominator. The per-viewer SE has √n in the denominator as is standard for a sample mean.

Winsorization narrows both CIs by reducing SD without materially changing the mean or rate.

§8 · Two windows

28-day and 7-day rates: independent estimates

28-day window primary estimate

Captures the full substitution cycle. Wider CIs empirically (~1.9× the 7d width) because delta variance grows over the longer window as viewing patterns diverge. Uses the same post-period that calibrated the matching model. Engagement weights are based on 28d on-title viewing shares.

7-day window sensitivity check

Captures immediate displacement only. Tighter CIs (less variance in short-window deltas). Less contaminated by subsequent title completions within the window. Engagement weights are independently recomputed from 7d on-title viewing shares. Agreement between 28d and 7d rates is a validity signal.

The 7d pipeline recomputes post-period TVT from video_session with a 7-day window, then runs the full delta → weight → winsorize → rate chain independently. It uses its own percentile bounds and its own engagement weights.

§9 · Worked example

A single title × segment cell

Title T, Power Viewer segment, 200 matched pairs, 28d window.

  1. Compute deltas and on-title TVT per pair.

    Each pair yields simple_delta_off_T (seconds) and tvt_on_T_post (seconds the treated viewer spent on title T in 28 days).

  2. Winsorize within the cell.

    Clip both quantities at [P1, P99] computed over the 200 pairs in this cell. Extreme viewers are bounded.

  3. Aggregate with weights.

    Suppose Σ(delta_wz × weight) = −360,000 sec and Σ(tvt_on_T_post_wz) = 2,400,000 sec. The displacement ratio = −360,000 / 2,400,000 = −0.15. Rate = 1 + (−0.15) = 0.85.

  4. Apply to April segment TVT.

    If this title had 50,000 hrs of Power Viewer TVT in April: incr_tvt = 0.85 × 50,000 = 42,500 hrs. Of the 50,000 hrs viewers spent on this title, 42,500 were net-new to the platform; 7,500 hrs were displaced from other content.

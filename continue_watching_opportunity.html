1. The Conversion Gap: Dwell vs. Resume by Position

Dwell Share vs. Resume Share

Positions 6+ attract a third of all user attention but convert at 1/4 the rate of the top positions. This is wasted real estate under the current recency-first ordering.

Key Insight: The current CW row is ordered primarily by recency. Position 0 (most recently watched) captures 62% of all resumes. But the long tail (positions 6+) shows that users actively scroll and browse — they spend 3.2 seconds per tile vs 1.1s at position 2 — yet rarely commit.

This isn't a discovery problem. Users see these titles. It's a relevance problem — the titles surfaced at tail positions aren't compelling enough to resume.

4. The Opportunity: What Re-Ranking Could Unlock

TVT Currently Stranded in Tail

Scenario: Move 20% of Tail Resumes to Top 3

If re-ranking moves even 20% of position 6+ resumes into positions 0-2 (by surfacing the right titles earlier), that's ~7,300 additional qualified resumes at top positions — where CTR is 8x higher.

Conservative estimate: Positions 6+ produce 36,714 qualified resumes (3% sample) over 7 weeks. If a predictive model could surface the "right" titles earlier, capturing even 20% of those would add ~4,270 hours of incremental TVT (at 34.9 min/resume) — moved from low-conversion positions to high-conversion ones where the halo effect on session length kicks in.

Conclusion

The Continue Watching container has a clear structural inefficiency:

  • 32% of user attention lands on positions 6+ but produces only 7% of resumes — a 4.4x gap.
  • Users aren't ignoring the tail — they dwell 3.2s per tile (vs 1.1s at position 2) — they're browsing with intent but not finding the right title.
  • When tail-position users DO commit, they watch 18% longer (34.9 min vs 29.6 min) — proving these are high-value sessions.
  • A predictive re-ranking model has 6 feature groups available (recency, ML score, fandom match, title attributes, user behavior, context) — all joinable at the (device, program, ds) grain.
  • Conservative upside: recovering 20% of tail resumes into top-3 positions ≈ +4,270 TVT hours over 7 weeks from the 3% sample alone. Extrapolated to full population: ~142,000 hours of incremental TVT.

Next step: Build a training dataset using the feature SQL, train a binary classifier (resumed ≥5 min), and evaluate lift vs. the recency-only baseline via offline replay.

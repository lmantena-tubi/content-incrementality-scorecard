What is Noise Share?

When we measure a title's incrementality rate on different days, the rate bounces around. Noise share tells us why it bounces:

Noise share ≈ 1.0 (noise-dominated)
The day-to-day bounce is just sampling randomness — like getting slightly different poll results with different samples. The true rate isn't actually changing. Fix: pool more days or pairs to average out the noise.
Noise share ≈ 0 (signal-dominated)
The day-to-day bounce reflects real variation — the true rate genuinely differs Monday vs. Wednesday (e.g., due to competing content). Pooling helps precision but won't eliminate the swings.

Formula: noise_share = avg(daily SE²) / observed_variance(daily rates). Valid only for the 1d post-window where daily estimates are non-overlapping. The 3d/7d windows overlap between consecutive dates, so their variance decomposition is invalid — we report rolling stability (SD, sign agreement) only.

Noise = noise_share > 0.9 · Mixed = 0.5–0.9 · Signal = < 0.5

Rate Stability Tiers (Signal CV)

Signal CV = Signal SD / |mean rate| — measures how much the true rate (after removing sampling noise) varies day-to-day relative to its level.

Low Drift
CV < 0.15, noise_share ≤ 0.9
Stable AND we can see through the noise.
Moderate drift
CV 0.15–0.40
Real variation exists but bounded.
High drift
CV > 0.40 or mean ≈ 0
Definitively unstable OR rate crosses zero.
Unresolved
CV < 0.15, noise_share > 0.9
Can't measure stability — need more pairs or longer pooling.

Signal SD = √max(0, SD² − avg_SE²) — the irreducible day-to-day variation after removing sampling noise.

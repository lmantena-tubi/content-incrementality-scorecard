Content Incrementality v6b — Forest Plot (Total Incremental TVT)

Top 50 titles by TVT × 4 segments · 28-day window · Total = on-title TVT + off-title incremental · 95% CI

Key takeaway: Content is incremental. Using the most conservative method (Additive Proportional), each title generates a median of 1.9 hours of total incremental viewing per viewer over 28 days — the on-title viewing itself plus its proportional share of off-title lift. 92% of title×segment combinations (184 of 200) are statistically significant. Simple and DiD methods, which attribute the full treated-vs-control gap to the title, show a median of 8.3 hours — but this overstates any single title's contribution when viewers watch multiple titles.

Matching quality is strong: Pre-period TVT gap is ~0 hours across all titles, and Simple ≈ DiD (4.58× vs 4.60×), confirming that treated and control groups had equivalent pre-period behavior. This means the observed differences are attributable to the title, not pre-existing viewer differences.

How the methods compare: All four methods estimate how much additional off-title viewing each hour of on-title viewing generates. They all use the control group as a counterfactual, but differ in scope and whether they use the pre-period.

Simple computes the post-period off-title TVT difference between treated and control viewers. Additive Proportional scales this by weight_T — the title's share of a viewer's total on-title viewing time (tvt_on_T / tvt_on_all_titles), typically 5–20%. This is the most conservative method because it prevents double-counting across titles.

DiD differences out each group's pre-to-post change to isolate the title's causal effect. DiD Additive Proportional applies weight_T to DiD. In v6b, with the pre-gap near zero, DiD and Simple agree — validating both approaches when matching is clean.

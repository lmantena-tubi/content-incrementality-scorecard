Content Incrementality v6 — Rate vs Pre-Period Gap (with 95% CI)

50 titles × 4 viewer segments · 1.83M matched pairs · 28-day window · Error bars = 95% confidence intervals · Filled = significant, hollow = not significant

Each method estimates a content incrementality rate — how much additional off-title viewing each hour of on-title viewing generates. A rate of 2× means every hour on a title is associated with 2 extra hours elsewhere. All four methods compare treated viewers against matched controls, but differ in what data they use.

Simple and Additive Proportional only use post-period data, making them immune to pre-period matching quality. Additive Proportional scales by the title's share of on-title viewing. Median rates: 8.1× and 1.4×.

DiD and DiD Additive Proportional also use the pre-period, differencing out each group's trajectory. In v6, treated viewers watched less than controls pre-period (negative pre-gap), inflating DiD rates via regression to the mean. The scatter plots show this: DiD rates correlate with pre-gap size (r = −0.78), while Simple/Add Prop show no such relationship.

Bottom line: Until matching closes the pre-period gap, Simple and Additive Proportional are more trustworthy.

Absolute Incremental TVT (hours per viewer)

These charts show the raw per-viewer incremental off-title hours (not divided by on-title TVT). Useful for understanding the absolute magnitude of the effect.

Simple — incremental off-title TVT per viewer (hours)
AVG(treated_post_off − control_post_off) in hours
DiD — incremental off-title TVT per viewer (hours)
AVG((treated_post − treated_pre) − (control_post − control_pre)) in hours
Incrementality Rates (per hour of on-title TVT)

These charts show rates: incremental hours divided by on-title TVT. A rate of 1× means break-even; above 1× means net-positive incrementality.

Simple rate
rate = 1 + Σ(treated_post_off − control_post_off) / Σon_title_tvt
Additive Proportional rate
rate = 1 + Σ(simple × weight_T) / Σon_title_tvt
DiD rate
rate = 1 + ΣDiD / Σon_title_tvt
DiD Additive Proportional rate
rate = 1 + Σ(DiD × weight_T) / Σon_title_tvt
Key takeaway: Error bars are tightest for Retained and New segments (large n), widest for Power Viewers (small n). Most Simple and DiD dots are significantly > 1. Additive Proportional — the most conservative method — shows many titles with CIs crossing 1. The absolute-hours charts confirm that even the smallest effects are several hours per viewer over 28 days.

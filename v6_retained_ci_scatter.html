Content Incrementality v6 — Retained Viewers

50 titles · Active non-power (Retained) segment · 28-day window · Error bars = 95% confidence intervals

Key takeaway: All four methods confirm positive incrementality for retained viewers, but they disagree on magnitude. Additive Proportional (median 1.29×) is the most conservative — it attributes only the title's proportional share of the total simple effect, so for titles that are a small fraction of a viewer's diet, the allocated lift is modest. Simple (median 4.71×) attributes the entire treated-vs-control gap to the title, which overstates any single title's contribution when viewers watch many titles. DiD (median 7.01×) and DiD Additive Proportional (median 2.27×) are inflated because treated viewers watched less than controls pre-period (negative pre-gap), causing regression-to-mean bias.

Recommendation: For retained viewers, Additive Proportional (1.29×) is the most defensible estimate — it uses the control group as counterfactual, ignores the problematic pre-period, and attributes only title T's fair share. However, 20% of titles show CIs crossing 1 (not significant), so confidence varies by title.

Why methods differ in conservatism: The four methods sit on a spectrum from most to least conservative. Additive Proportional is most conservative because it takes the simple treated-vs-control gap and multiplies by weight_T — the title's share of a viewer's total on-title viewing time, i.e. tvt_on_T / tvt_on_all_titles. This weight is typically 5–20%, so it attributes only title T's proportional share of the effect. This prevents double-counting when a viewer watches many titles. Simple assigns the full gap to the title — fine if we want "total ecosystem lift associated with title T viewers" but not a per-title causal claim. DiD methods attempt causal identification by removing pre-existing trends, but in v6 the negative pre-gap (treated watched less than controls before) inflates DiD via regression to the mean — the treated group's "improvement" partly reflects natural reversion, not title-driven lift.

The scatter plots below show each title against its pre-period TVT gap. Notice that Simple and Add Prop show no correlation with pre-gap (they ignore pre-period data), while DiD rates climb as the pre-gap becomes more negative — a signature of regression-to-mean bias.

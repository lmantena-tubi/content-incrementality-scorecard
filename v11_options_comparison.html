Series event-definition options A–D

Content Incrementality v11 · Top-2000 US SERIES · events Aug 8–14 2026 · 7-day post · Coverage, duplication, leak profile, complexity — measured, not asserted
What this decides. When does a viewer's session on a show count as a treated event? All four options are thresholds on one quantity — the gap between the event and the viewer's last prior session on the same show (any episode, show grain). They sit on an identical candidate pool of 5,945,788 (viewer × show) pairs, so every comparison below is strict pool inclusion: nothing is gained by one option that another cannot also see. Option B ships today. Every figure on this page comes from v11_coverage_* / v11_dup_* / v11_series_watch_* on Databricks.

A  ⊆  C  ⊆  B  ⊆  D
The structural fact that reframes C. For New (M1) and Non-active (Reactivated), C is byte-identical to B — B's own branch for those segments is already 28d (both read pct_vs_option_b = 100). C's entire 21.2% pool loss therefore lands on Power Viewer (−27.6%) and Retained (−22.0%). "C is uniform across segments" is true, but it only changes two of four segments — and those two are the highest-TVT, best-matched cells.

2 · Why this is a series question: series vs movie watching behaviour

Every screen exists to keep continuation out of the treated definition. So the first question is how much continuation there is. Decomposing US TVT Aug 8–14 by the gap to the device's previous day on the same title (3% sample, device_id × title × day, 180d lookback):

Series viewing is 80.2% continuation; movies are 23.8%. That single contrast is why the movie pipeline needs no treated screen and the series pipeline does. Under option D — the "movie parity" choice — a series treated event is a re-watch or a mid-binge continuation four times out of five, where for movies it is a genuine first encounter three times out of four. D is parity in code, not parity in meaning.

The exact intra-day read confirms how tight series continuation is: 81.8% of series TVT sits in a session that began less than an hour after the previous session on the same show (v11_series_watch_intraday, B_video_session_exact). Series continuation is not a weekly habit — it is the next episode, minutes later.

Three things follow directly, and they drive every number in the rest of this report.

  • Series Power Viewers are 60.4% of series TVT and 87.2% continuation. Any prior-viewing screen is overwhelmingly a screen on this one cell. That is the mechanical reason A and C shed most of their pool from Power Viewer, and why the segment-conditional design in B exists at all.
  • The 8–28d bucket is 6.85% of series TVT — the behavioural size of what B's 7d branch lets through (§5 measures the same quantity from the pair side: 23.92% of B's kept TVT). The >28d bucket is 4.95% — the mid-season-resume band that C also misses and only A catches.
  • New (M1) and Reactivated barely continue at all in the near bands (New: 3.0% at 8–28d, 0% beyond; Reactivated: 0% inside 28d by construction, 23.6% at >28d). A 28d screen on those segments is close to free — which is exactly why C == B there.

4 · Duplication — the flip side of coverage

A looser screen means a viewer is treated on more titles, so their post-window TVT enters the incremental sum more times. The multiplicity is k = titles per viewer surviving the screen. The quantity that inflates a pair-weighted sum is pair-weighted mean k = Σk²·nk / Σk·nk (always ≥ mean k), and w_invk = AVG(1/k) is the matching equal-split correction weight.

Monotone with looseness, as the nesting requires: A 1.853 → C 1.963 → B 2.124 → D 2.600. Under D, 59.7% of pairs belong to a viewer treated on 2+ titles and Power Viewers average 3.34 — one viewer's post hours counted more than three times. Under A that falls to 2.20.

The honest correction to an earlier argument. "Option C avoids duplication" is not a valid reason to prefer C. The published pipeline already de-duplicates via the step-10 TSWEIGHT ladder (pooled 0.145–0.476 by segment, i.e. it removes 52–86% of the raw sum). Tightening the screen and applying the weight double-corrects. C's genuine selling point is cross-segment uniformity — one rule, no segment-conditional confound — not duplication avoidance.

The ladder already over-corrects by 1.33×–2.27× pooled, and 1.75×–4.74× for Power Viewer (by segment: Retained 2.12× / 1.24×, New 2.23× / 1.29×, Reactivated 1.80× / 1.46×). This matters for the option choice in a direction that is easy to get backwards:

  • Moving to A or C reduces multiplicity, so the correct weight rises toward 1 — widening the existing over-correction unless the ladder is refitted. A tighter screen makes the shipped weight more wrong, not less.
  • Moving to D raises multiplicity, so the correct weight falls — nudging the ladder toward correctness while adding the most contamination. The two effects point in opposite directions; neither alone decides.
  • Staying on B keeps a known, sized, signed bias. That is the cheapest defensible position, and it is what the methodology doc should state rather than imply neutrality.

4.2 Three grains of k — measure on the wrong one and you flatter the weight

The catalog sum multiplies Δ/viewer by full-population viewer counts, so the multiplicity to correct for is titles-per-viewer in that population. Measuring on matched pairs loses titles to the ML join and the 28% match failure, and because 1/k is convex that overstates the correct weight:

7 · Pros and cons, side by side

A · Lifetime (180d)

Keep only if no on-title session in 180 days.

Pros

  • Closest to a true "first encounter" — the only option that closes the 29–180d resume band (16.18% of B's TVT, 28.84% for Reactivated).
  • Lowest duplication of all four: pw mean k 1.853, 43.2% of pairs k≥2, Power Viewer 2.199.
  • One uniform rule across segments.
  • Cleanest causal story for a discovery claim.

Cons

  • Sheds 36.3% of B's pool and 41.5% of its TVT — the harshest coverage cost by far.
  • Cost is concentrated where the TVT is: Power Viewer −42.2%, Retained −37.7%.
  • Heaviest implementation — a new 180d history scan beyond the existing 28d window.
  • Reduces multiplicity most, so it widens the already 1.33–2.27× over-correcting weight unless the ladder is refitted.
  • "Lifetime" is a misnomer: it is "not in 180d." Series with long hiatuses still leak.
  • Fewest cells clear n_pairs ≥ 30; thinnest per-title reporting.

B · Segment-conditional 7d/28d ships

Gap > 7d (Power, Retained) or > 28d (New, Reactivated).

Pros

  • Best coverage of the three screened options: 62.8% of candidate pairs, 44.2% of candidate TVT.
  • Screen window mirrors the per-segment matching window — internally coherent by design.
  • Zero code cost; reuses the existing 28d engagement scan. All published numbers and the TSWEIGHT ladder are already calibrated to it.
  • Its bias is measured and signed (leak 23.92% of TVT; weight over-corrects 1.33–2.27×) — a known quantity beats an unquantified alternative.
  • Middling duplication: pw mean k 2.124, between C's 1.963 and D's 2.600.

Cons

  • Two rules, not one. Hardest of the four to explain, and it confounds the segment cut with the event definition — the segment both selects the rule and is the reporting dimension.
  • Leaks 23.92% of its kept TVT (Power Viewer 30.15%) to on-title watching 8–28 days before the event: a power viewer who watched the show 10 days ago still clears the screen.
  • Misses the 29–180d resume band entirely (16.18% of TVT).
  • Threshold incoherence: the event bar is 5 minutes but the screen fires on any TVT > 0, so a 90-second accidental start disqualifies a viewer a 5-minute session would have qualified.

C · Fixed 28d

Gap > 28d, every segment.

Pros

  • One rule, one number. Removes the segment-conditional confound — this is C's real and only structural advantage.
  • Closes B's 8–28d leak exactly: the 23.92%-of-TVT band (30.15% for Power Viewer) goes to zero.
  • Cheapest change of the three alternatives — one literal plus deleting a branch.
  • Costs nothing for New (M1) and Reactivated: C == B there, so half the segments are untouched.
  • Lower duplication than B (pw mean k 1.963 vs 2.124) and lower clustering penalty.

Cons

  • Costs 21.2% of pairs / 25.2% of TVT, and because New and Reactivated are unaffected, the entire loss lands on Power Viewer (−27.6%) and Retained (−22.0%) — the highest-TVT, best-matched cells, 93.6% of series TVT between them.
  • Withdrawn argument "Avoids duplication" is not a valid pro — the TSWEIGHT ladder already de-duplicates. Applying both double-corrects, and since the ladder over-corrects by 1.33–2.27× today, C makes that worse.
  • Still misses the 29–180d resume band (16.18% of TVT) — the same blind spot as B.
  • 28 days is as arbitrary as 7; it inherits the matching window's number without a behavioural justification of its own.
  • Requires a full pipeline re-run plus a refitted weight to be reportable.

D · No screen (v10e movie parity)

No prior-viewing test at all.

Pros

  • Exact code parity with the movie pipeline — movies and series become comparable on the event definition, one fewer methodology delta to explain.
  • Maximum coverage: the full 5,945,788-pair candidate pool, 2.26× B's TVT.
  • No screen to explain, no threshold to defend, zero new code.
  • Most pairs clear n_pairs ≥ 30 — most reportable cells, tightest CIs.
  • Highest multiplicity means the correct de-dup weight is smallest, which happens to move the existing ladder toward correctness rather than away.

Cons

  • Maximum contamination, and for series that is the whole ballgame: series TVT is 80.2% continuation vs movies' 23.8% (§2). Under D a series "treated event" is usually a re-watch or a mid-binge continuation, so the measured effect is substantially continuation, not incremental discovery.
  • Series Power Viewers — 60.4% of series TVT and 87.2% continuation — are almost entirely unscreened.
  • Highest duplication: pw mean k 2.600, 59.7% of pairs k≥2, 11.7% at k≥5, max 66; Power Viewer 3.343.
  • Largest clustering penalty on standard errors and the largest gap between treated and control on prior on-title exposure (controls are screened to zero by construction, so the asymmetry is widest here).
  • Parity in code is not parity in meaning — the same rule measures different things on the two catalogs, which arguably makes the movie/series comparison less valid, not more.

How to read this as a decision. The behaviour data (§2) settles one thing outright: a series pipeline needs some screen — 80.2% continuation against movies' 23.8% means D's movie-parity rule measures a different construct on series than it does on movies. Among the three screened options the trade is one-dimensional and explicit: A buys the cleanest discovery definition at 41.5% of candidate TVT; C buys cross-segment uniformity and closes B's largest leak for 25.2% of candidate TVT, all of it from Power Viewer and Retained; B buys coverage and zero cost while carrying a leak and a weight bias that are now both measured. Any move off B also requires refitting the TSWEIGHT ladder — otherwise the change trades a sized bias for an unsized one.

9 · Caveats on these numbers

  • Funnel-grain absolutes read ~2% low. Buckets 7+8 = 3,733,698 vs phase2_treated_v11 = 3,826,770, because v11_coverage.sql anchors on fq.ts rather than DATE_TRUNC('minute', fq.ts). Ratios and shares are sound; absolute pair counts are ~2% under.
  • Pool sizing is not a re-estimate. §3–§5 size pools, coverage and leaks on the shared candidate pool. Nobody has re-run matching under A, C or D, so no option's Δ/viewer or Incremental TVT is known. Direction of Δ under a looser screen is two-sided (added pairs carry prior exposure, which inflates Δ; they are also habitual watchers with higher counterfactuals, which deflates it) — a previous directional prediction of this kind was wrong by +50%.
  • Behaviour is device_id-grain, 3% sample (device_watch_daily), while the pipeline is viewer_id-grain on video_session. Shares are robust; upsampled device counts (×33.3) are estimates. The 180d lookback means "new" strictly means "not in 180 days."
  • Post-window coverage (§3.5) is measured on all Aug 8–14 events, so its 28d figures are partly censored for the later event days; the 7d figures are complete.
  • Control-pool logic is identical across all four options — no option changes the control screen, so the treated/control asymmetry on prior on-title exposure widens monotonically with looseness (widest under D) and is not fixable by CEM, since no control with on-title history exists to block against.
  • v11_dup_window_overlap labels its pooled rows user_segment = 'unknown' rather than 'all' — cosmetic, no numeric effect.

Revision 2 did exactly what it was designed to do, and it was not close to enough. The two changes — treated cut to 1.5% while control stayed at 3%, and the universe cut from top-2000 to top-500 — were aimed at the cell-supply channel. Measured: control devices per (title × date) in the pres=1 stratum rose 4.2× (966 → 4,104 at the median), in-cell controls per treated rose ~1.5×, and the empty-cell rate roughly halved. The overall match rate moved 32.70% → 36.71% — +4.0 pp against a 43 pp shortfall. The reason is in §3: the dominant loss channel is the propensity caliper, which consumes a near-constant 37–50% of treated in every stratum and is not a supply problem. Per the locked decision: report and stop.

What this pair of runs now establishes, that revision 1 alone could not. Revision 1 left open the possibility that the shortfall was a sampling artefact — fix the supply, clear the gate. Revision 2 is the controlled test of that hypothesis and it falsifies it: a 4.2× supply increase bought 4 pp. The binding constraint is the treated–control propensity overlap (§4), where the medians sit 0.93–1.44 logits apart against a ±0.2 caliper. No sampling change inside this design reaches 80%.

Both sides rose — the top-500 universe is more heavily merchandised, so presentation is more common on both treated and control. The ratio improved from 11.2× to 9.3×, which is real but second-order. Exact-blocking still points 78% of demand at 8.4% of supply. Presentation is a cause of viewing, so the treated cohort is intrinsically enriched in "was shown the title" — the block is partially confounded with the treatment itself, and that does not change with sampling.

The 78.10% is a universe effect, not a defect, and the control for that claim is had_pres_strict: it measured 42.67% on top-500 against the 43.2% top-2000 benchmark — i.e. it reproduced. So the entire 62.6% → 78.1% shift lives in the +12h post-anchor tail, which is where merchandised titles differ most. Do not read 78.1% as a broken flag.

The treated median sits 0.93–1.44 logits above the control median in every stratum — 4.7 to 7.2 caliper-widths against a ±0.2 caliper. The matchable region is only where the treated bottom decile overlaps the control top decile (pres=0 Retained: treated p10 −1.280 vs control p90 −0.836 — the overlap is a sliver). Revision 1's sub-sampled estimate gave 0.91–1.38; the exact figures are 0.93–1.44. The sub-sample was not misleading, and the geometry did not move when the universe and sample changed.

This remains a framework-level property, not a v13 quirk. The overlap gap is expected — the ML score's job is to predict viewing, so viewers score higher than non-viewers by construction. But it means the caliper has always been matching inside a thin overlap tail. v10e cleared 83.8% on that same tail only because a 100% control sample put enough bodies in it. Revision 2 put 4.2× more bodies into each cell and the tail still could not fill, because the cell and the tail are different constraints and only the first one was relaxed.

The revision-1 inversion reproduces: median shift is smaller for pres=1 (0.93–1.23) than pres=0 (1.13–1.44). Conditioning on presentation improves propensity overlap — presentation and high score are both "the ranker surfaced this" — while costing far more in cell supply. The block helps the channel it was not aimed at and hurts the one it was.

5. The had_pres_strict residual — an unblocked imbalance worth naming

had_pres_strict (presentation strictly before the anchor) is deliberately not blocked — only had_pres is. So this gap is a free diagnostic, and it points the opposite way from what a naive reading would expect: matched controls are more likely than their treated partners to have had a strictly-prior presentation. The mechanism is the +12h tail — a treated viewer's had_pres can be satisfied by a presentation logged after their event_ts, whereas a control anchored at midnight has less post-anchor room inside the same window. It is a concrete measurement of the post-treatment contamination the plan flagged as a caveat, now quantified on final pairs rather than only on the preflight: roughly 62% of treated had_pres=1 flags are not strictly prior. Any causal reading of the block must carry this.

The gate is unreachable by caliper relaxation. Removing the caliper entirely — an infinite ceiling, matching on the nine exact blocks alone — yields 79.62% on pres=1, which still misses 80%. The asymptote is set by the 17.47% of treated whose exact cell contains no control at any distance (46,054 rows), and 17.47% + 79.62%-of-the-rest is all there is. Every caliper value in the table is a trade of balance for volume that never arrives at the target.

Three things the curve says, beyond the headline:

  • The first 0.1 of relaxation is the cheapest and it is still not enough. 0.2→0.3 buys +6.07 pp overall (+5.81 pp on pres=1) — the largest single increment in the table. Returns then decay monotonically: +4.75, +3.77, +3.34, +2.94, +2.57, +2.31, +2.04 pp. Getting pres=1 merely to 60% takes a caliper of 1.0 — five times the current ceiling. Getting overall to 80% takes 3.0, fifteen times, and pres=1 is at 77.19% even there.
  • Balance degrades faster than volume improves. The ≤0.1 tier-1 share — the fraction of pairs the stepwise rank actually prefers — falls 74.47% → 42.38% by caliper 1.0, and mean pair distance rises 0.063 → 0.259, a 4.1× widening. The pairs added by relaxation are much worse than the ones already there: newly-admitted pairs average 0.247 logits apart at caliper 0.3, 0.332 at 0.5 and 0.519 at 1.0. For scale, the treated–control median logit shift measured in §4 is 0.93–1.44 — so a caliper of 1.0 admits most of the raw offset the score exists to remove, and the design stops adjusting on propensity in any meaningful sense.
  • Reactivated is structural, as it was in v10e. Its pres=1 ceiling is 46.54% at infinite caliper — 24.82% today. No caliper reaches even 50%. This is the same ~73%-tops-out control-supply limit documented for the frozen framework, made worse by the 1.5% treated cut and the presentation block. Power Viewer tops out at 71.67%; only Retained (88.43%) has a ceiling above the gate.

This table is a diagnostic, not a proposal. The locked decision in the plan is explicit — “no bin loosening, no caliper widening, no softening the block into a rank key” — and the measurement independently argues the same way: the one caliper that clears the gate does not exist, and every value that moves the number materially also destroys the balance the caliper is there to protect. Reported because “would a wider caliper have fixed it” is the first question anyone will ask of the 36.71%, and it now has a measured answer of no rather than an assumed one.

What revision 2 bought, in one line

+4.0 pp of match rate and a 19× reduction in reuse-cap breach severity, for a 4.2× supply increase. Empty-cell loss fell 3–8 pp per stratum; caliper loss absorbed most of it and rose in three strata. The reuse breach dropped from max 33× / 11.67% of controls to max 14× / 3.73%, with excess pairs down from 77,075-involved to 4,347 excess (4.49% of pairs) — the one gate that is now close to defensible. Nothing in the revision touched the propensity-overlap geometry, which is where 37–50% of every stratum is lost.

Open items

Decisions

  • Run steps 5–6 to publish the real 9-check report documenting the failure? That report is the stated deliverable of the plan. Needs v13_balance_diagnostics.sql (SQL warehouse) then notebooks/v13_diagnostics.py (cluster; clear stale .pkl from dbfs:/FileStore/phase2_diagnostics_v13 before refitting). It would also finally measure the two SMD gates, which are the only gates still unread.
  • Is a presentation-blocked design viable at all? Revision 2 is the evidence that the answer is not "more supply." Two options change the arithmetic rather than tweaking it:
    • Presentation as a term inside the propensity score rather than an exact block. Keeps the information, stops fragmenting cells, and — per §4's inversion, where pres=1 has better overlap — may improve the caliper channel rather than just dodging the cell channel. This is the option the measurements point at. It is a different design, not a loosening, and needs its own spec + sweep.
    • A 100%-sample presentation source. The ÷33 on controls is forced solely by viewpres_vidsession_sample being 3%. But §3 now shows supply is not the binding channel, so this is the weaker of the two — it would raise in-cell counts again and convert more of the loss into caliper loss.
  • Widening the caliper is not on this list and is explicitly ruled out by the plan ("no bin loosening, no caliper widening, no softening the block into a rank key"). §4 is also the reason it would not be a fix but a corruption: at a 0.93–1.44 median shift, a caliper wide enough to clear 80% would be pairing treated with controls 5–7 caliper-widths away in propensity.

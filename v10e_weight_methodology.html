Pre-TVT Bucket Weights — Selection Methodology

v10e · Content Incrementality · Top 2000 US Movies · Event window Aug 8–21 2026, 7-day post · How the incremental-TVT de-duplication weights are chosen
What this document decides. How each viewer's incremental TVT is weighted when summing across titles, so the catalog-wide total is not double-counted. The weight is read off the viewer's 7-day pre-event TVT via a single shared step ladder applied identically to every segment — two viewers with the same pre-TVT get the same weight regardless of segment — with lower weight for heavier pre-period watchers. The ladder is applied per matched pair and averaged within each (title × segment), so the effective weight is computed from each movie's own matched-viewer mix rather than a single flat number shared by all 2000 titles. This page explains the problem, why pre-TVT was chosen over the alternatives, which buckets are used, the weight per bucket, and how it rolls up to a per-title weight.

1 · The problem the weight solves

A treated viewer's incremental TVT for a title is (their post-window Tubi TVT) − (their matched control's). But a viewer who watches continuously is a treated viewer for every title they touch, so their whole post-window TVT block is counted once per title. Summed across 2000 titles, the same hours are counted many times over.

The symptom: the unweighted catalog-wide overall rate (Incr TVT ÷ measured title TVT) comes out at ≈186% — more than 100% of all TVT that actually happened — which is only possible because post hours are shared across titles. The weight is a de-duplication / apportionment factor, not a causal adjustment (the matched-pair Δ already handles the counterfactual).

Today's weights (Power Viewer 0.2, all other segments 0.5) are a crude stand-in for this: 0.2 ≈ 1/5 and 0.5 ≈ 1/2 are segment-average guesses at "how many titles does this viewer spread across." This proposal replaces the segment-average guess with the viewer's own pre-period engagement.

2 · Why pre-TVT — and why not the alternatives

Three de-duplication schemes were considered. Pre-TVT bucketing was chosen because it is the only one that is simultaneously simple to explain, period-independent, and free of delta-sign bias.

ApproachIdeaWhy rejected / chosen
Title-count split 1/n Divide each viewer's incremental TVT evenly across the titles they watched. rejected n grows with the observation window → period-dependent; longer windows mechanically shrink weights.
Rate / engagement-share attribution Credit each title a share of the viewer proportional to TVT-on-title. rejected Creates bias for negative-delta titles — the share interacts with the sign of the effect and distorts titles that reduce viewing.
Pre-TVT bucket weight Weight each viewer by how much they watched before the event: heavy pre-watchers' added TVT is less likely net-new, so it is discounted. chosen Uses only the viewer's own pre-period TVT — absolute hours (period-independent), independent of the title or its delta sign (no negative-title bias), and it's a flat lookup (no decay/exponential to explain).

One-sentence rationale: "The less a viewer watched in the 7 days before, the more their added viewing counts as truly new — so we weight low-pre-TVT viewers up and heavy watchers down, reading the weight off one shared 7d pre-TVT ladder that treats every segment the same."

3 · Which buckets — one shared 7-day pre-TVT ladder

The weight keys off a single shared pre-TVT scale applied identically to every segment: the viewer's 7-day pre-event TVT (tvt_7d_hrs, carried for every treated viewer in phase2_treated_v10e). Same pre-TVT ⇒ same weight, whatever the segment.

Why a shared 7d scale, not the CEM tvt_bucket. The CEM matcher's tvt_bucket is segment-conditional and mixes pre-windows — Power Viewer & Retained are binned on 7-day pre-TVT, but New (M1) & Reactivated on 28-day pre-TVT. That makes its buckets incomparable across segments (a "20h" Power bucket is 20h/7d; a "20h" New bucket would be 20h/28d), which is exactly why the same bucket label can't carry the same weight. Keying every segment off the same 7d window fixes that. We use the 7d window (not 28d) because the double-counting we are de-duplicating happens over the 7-day post window — 7d pre-TVT is the most direct proxy for how many titles a viewer's post hours spread across.

4 · Weights per bucket & measured effect

One flat weight per bucket, identical across all four segments, monotonically decreasing with 7d pre-TVT. Zero-pre-TVT viewers count at full weight; the heaviest pre-watchers are discounted hardest. The segment does not appear anywhere in the lookup — only the viewer's own 7d pre-TVT. This is the per-viewer ladder; §4's callout below explains how it rolls up to a per-title weight.

The weight is applied per (title × segment), not per segment. The ladder above is the per-viewer lookup; the number the dashboard actually applies is computed per movie. For every matched pair we bin the treated viewer's own 7d pre-TVT on this ladder, read its weight, and take the plain average of those pair weights within each (title_id, user_segment) — each matched viewer counting equally (viewer-count-weighted). So a horror B-movie whose matched viewers are mostly zero-pre-TVT carries a higher effective weight than a tentpole whose viewers are heavy pre-watchers, even inside the same segment. This per-title weight is the TSWEIGHT[title_id][seg_idx] map the dashboard keys on (step10e → phase3_title_segment_applied_weight_v10e). The flat per-segment numbers below are the pair-weighted average of those per-title weights — kept only as the dashboard's fallback for any (title × segment) missing from the map.

Why AVG(w_pair) and not Σ(w·δ)/Σ(δ): averaging the pair weights keeps the number in the ladder's own range [0.04, 0.50] and never blows up. The Δ-weighted alternative (total weighted incr ÷ total incr) explodes or goes negative for titles whose net incremental Σδ is near zero or negative — a display artifact, not a real weight.

Pooled effect vs. today (from the step10 run). Rolled back up across all titles, the aggregate move for each segment comes from where that segment sits on the 7d pre-TVT scale. Note — the halved ladder now sits below today's constants for every segment. This ladder de-duplicates more aggressively than the current 0.2 / 0.5: Reactivated/New/Retained fall from 0.50 to 0.32–0.44, and Power Viewer drops from 0.20 to 0.14. The halving was applied to pull the catalog-wide overall rate down toward today's level. Whether it is correctly calibrated depends on how much of these viewers' post hours are genuinely title-attributable vs. shared across a continuous watch — the single CASE in Section 2 of the notebook is the tuning surface, and the effective-weight table above is the check to re-run after any edit.

The v10e_step10 notebook is read-only against upstream. It reads tvt_7d_hrs from phase2_treated_v10e and bins every segment on one shared 7d ladder, producing the ladder itself:

The v10e_step10e_weights notebook then drops from the per-viewer ladder to the per-(title × segment) grain. It joins phase2_event_deltas_v10e (one row per matched pair, carries title_id, user_segment, treated_viewer_id) to phase2_treated_v10e (for tvt_7d_hrs), bins each pair on the shared ladder, and averages the pair weights within each (title × segment):

1 — Per-title weightphase3_title_segment_applied_weight_v10e One row per (title_id, seg_idx): applied_weight = AVG(w_pair) over that movie's matched pairs. The TSWEIGHT source (~8K rows).

The per-title map is emitted by build_results_data_v10e.py as const TSWEIGHT = {title_id: {seg_idx: weight}}; straight from Section 1's table. The dashboard's incremental sum then changes only its weight source — from four segment constants to the per-title TSWEIGHT lookup (with the pooled SEG_WEIGHT as fallback) — keeping the formula Incr = Σ(Δ/viewer × viewers × weight), only now weight is title-specific.

6 · Why this scheme is defensible

RequirementMet?How
Simple / explainable✓A lookup table, not a curve — read in ten seconds.
Lower weight for heavier watchers✓Weight is monotonically decreasing in 7d pre-TVT.
Same ladder across segments✓Segment never enters the per-viewer lookup — two viewers with equal 7d pre-TVT get the same ladder weight. The per-title effective weight then differs by movie because the matched-viewer mix differs.
Not period-dependent✓Cutoffs are absolute pre-period hours; no title counting.
No negative-title bias✓Weight depends only on the viewer's own pre-TVT, never on the title or its delta sign.
No decay / exponential✓Flat within each tier, stepping down across tiers.

Accepted trade-off. Keying every segment off one shared 7d pre-TVT scale means we do not reuse the CEM tvt_bucket verbatim (it mixes 7d and 28d pre-windows, so its bucket labels aren't comparable across segments). We instead cut fresh, comparable 7d bins from tvt_7d_hrs — already carried for every treated viewer. This is the cost of one segment-neutral ladder; it remains a plain lookup and touches no upstream table.
